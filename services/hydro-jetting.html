Hydro-jetting & pipe cleaning

Why jetting instead of snaking

A mechanical cable cuts a channel through an obstruction. Flow returns, the customer is happy, and the underlying condition — a wall coated in grease, a mat of fine roots, a layer of mineral scale — is still there, catching the next thing that comes down the line. That is why snaked lines so often back up again on a predictable cycle.

Hydro-jetting uses high-pressure water through a nozzle that directs most of its jets rearward. Those jets do two things at once: they pull the hose forward through the line, and they scour the pipe wall in every direction as they travel. The debris is flushed back toward the access point rather than pushed further downstream. What you get back is the pipe’s actual inside diameter, not a hole through the middle of the problem.

What it clears

  • Roots. Fine root mats that a cable tears rather than removes. Jetting strips them back to the wall.
  • Grease and soap scale. The dominant failure mode in restaurant and multi-family lines. Cables slide straight through grease without removing it.
  • Mineral and struvite scale. Hard deposits that progressively choke capacity.
  • Sand, silt, and gravel. Common in lines that have taken storm intrusion.
  • Construction debris. Grout, mortar, and the assorted contents of a line that was left open during a build.

Inspect first, then jet

We recommend a CCTV inspection before jetting whenever the pipe’s condition is unknown. That is not an upsell — it is the honest sequence. High-pressure water in a structurally compromised line can turn a hairline crack into a hole, and it will find a badly deteriorated clay or Orangeburg pipe the hard way. Knowing what you are working on lets us select pressure and nozzle appropriately, or tell you that the line needs repair rather than cleaning.

Jetting also does not fix geometry. If a line has a belly holding water or a sag from a failed bedding, cleaning restores capacity temporarily but the low spot will keep collecting solids. The camera tells you which problem you actually have.

Post-jetting verification

A second camera pass after cleaning shows the wall is genuinely clear and reveals defects that debris was hiding. On a new development, that after-video is the documentation that the line was delivered clean — useful when the question of who owns a downstream blockage comes up later. Post-construction validation bundles the two into a single acceptance deliverable.

Preventive scheduling

Grease-heavy lines and root-prone laterals benefit from a maintenance interval rather than emergency calls. A scheduled jetting is a planned cost; a Friday-night backup in an occupied building is not.

What we need on site

Access to a cleanout or manhole, and a water source unless the unit is arriving with a full tank. We will confirm both when we scope the job so there are no surprises on arrival.
