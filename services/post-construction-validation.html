Post-construction validation

The window to find a problem in newly installed pipe is short. It closes when the trench is backfilled, and it slams shut when the street is paved and the maintenance bond is released. After that, a defect the installing contractor caused becomes the owner’s repair — at full excavation cost, through new asphalt.

Validation is a CCTV inspection of the completed work, coded to the NASSCO PACP standard, performed while the responsible party is still contractually on the hook. It is cheap relative to what it protects.

What it typically catches

  • Construction debris left in the line — grout, gravel, bedding material, and the occasional glove.
  • Crushed or deformed pipe from equipment tracking over an under-covered run.
  • Improperly seated or offset joints that will infiltrate the moment groundwater rises.
  • Sags and bellies from inadequate or washed-out bedding — visible as standing water in the video.
  • Taps and service connections cut at the wrong angle or left protruding into the flow.
  • Missing or misplaced segments where the as-built and the actual installation diverge.

Why it matters to each party

Developers get documentation that the infrastructure they are turning over meets spec. General contractors get proof their subcontractor performed, before the sub demobilizes. Engineers get a coded record that supports certification without relying on visual spot-checks. Municipalities and utilities accepting the system get an independently produced baseline for the asset they are about to own.

Pre-design surveys

The other end of the project. Before an engineer designs a tie-in, a rehabilitation, or a new run through an existing corridor, somebody has to establish what is actually down there — not what the record drawings from 1974 claim is down there.

A pre-design survey documents existing pipe material and diameter, structural condition, apparent grade and flow direction, active service connections, and accessible structures. Where mapping matters, camera work pairs with above-ground locating so observations land at real coordinates rather than approximate footage marks.

The purpose is to eliminate the expensive surprise. A design built on assumed conditions produces change orders the first week of construction; a design built on verified conditions does not.

Fiber and low-voltage conduit

The same logic applies to duct. Before a fiber pull, verifying that conduit is continuous, unobstructed, and free of collapse or intruding debris is far cheaper than discovering the obstruction mid-pull with a crew and a reel standing idle. Post-installation, validation documents that the duct bank was delivered in the condition the contract specified.

This is a core part of QPI’s work rather than a sideline — fiber optics, low-voltage conduit, and residential utility prep are the sectors we are built around.

How this fits with our other services

Validation is a CCTV inspection performed at a specific contractual moment, with the report framed for acceptance rather than diagnosis. Lines carrying construction debris usually need jetting before the camera run produces anything meaningful. Where a system is suspected of taking water it should not, smoke and dye testing answers a question the camera cannot.

Scheduling

Validation has to be booked against your construction schedule, not ours — after cleanup, before paving, and with enough margin that a finding can still be corrected without re-mobilizing. Call us with your critical dates and we will work backward from them.
