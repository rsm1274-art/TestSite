Smoke & dye testing

The problem these tests solve

Inflow and infiltration — I&I in the trade — is clean water entering a sanitary system it has no business being in. Inflow arrives fast and obviously: a downspout tied into the sanitary line, an uncapped cleanout, a manhole taking surface runoff. Infiltration is slower and sneakier: groundwater seeping through cracked pipe, failed joints, and deteriorated service connections.

Either way you pay for it. On a private system you are pumping, treating, or surcharging water that should have gone to the storm side. On a development, an I&I problem discovered after acceptance becomes an argument about who owns it. And a system that backs up only during heavy rain will never be diagnosed correctly by a camera on a dry day.

How smoke testing works

We introduce a non-toxic, non-staining smoke into the line under low pressure with a blower and then watch where it comes out. Sanitary sewer is supposed to be a closed system: smoke should exit at roof vents and nowhere else. Anywhere else it emerges — from a lawn, a foundation crack, a storm inlet, an abandoned service line, a driveway seam — is a defect with a physical address.

That is the value. Smoke testing does not just tell you a system leaks; it walks you to the exact spot, above ground, where you can stand and photograph it. Each finding gets documented with location and photo so the repair scope writes itself.

Smoke testing is a surface-visible method, which means it requires notice. Residents and occupants need to know in advance that they may see smoke, that it is harmless, and that smoke entering a building usually indicates a dry floor drain trap or a defect worth reporting.

How dye testing works

Dye testing is the targeted follow-up. We introduce a coloured, non-toxic tracer dye at a specific point — a downspout, a floor drain, a suspected cross-connection, a pool overflow — and then observe where it appears downstream. If dye poured into a storm structure shows up in the sanitary manhole, you have proven the connection rather than inferred it.

Where smoke testing surveys broadly, dye testing confirms narrowly. It is the method that turns “we think this roof drain is tied in” into documentation you can put in front of a property owner or a contractor.

What you get

  • Defect log with location, type, and photo for every smoke emission point.
  • Confirmed cross-connections proven by dye, not assumed.
  • Segment-level summary so you can prioritize repairs by severity and cost.
  • Recommended follow-up — which segments justify a CCTV inspection to see the defect from the inside before you price the repair.

Conditions that affect results

Smoke testing works best on a dry day with modest wind. Saturated ground can trap smoke and mask defects; high wind disperses it before an observer sees it. Recent heavy rain is the worst case — the soil itself becomes a seal. We schedule around weather rather than burning your budget on a test that will under-report.

Lines carrying heavy debris may also need cleaning first so smoke can travel the full segment.
